BACKCOUNTRY AVALANCHE FORECAST FOR THE OLYMPICS, WASHINGTON
CASCADES AND MT HOOD AREA
These forecasts apply to back country avalanche terrain below
7000 feet. They do not apply to highways or operating ski areas.
**********************************************************
9 AM PST Friday, March 22, 2002
**********************************************************
ZONE AVALANCHE FORECASTS.....

OLYMPICS... WASHINGTON CASCADES EAST OF THE CREST...
MT HOOD AREA...

Considerable avalanche danger above about 6-7000 feet and
moderate below Friday. Avalanche danger decreasing Saturday
becoming moderate below 7000 feet.

WASHINGTON CASCADES NEAR AND WEST OF THE CREST...

Considerable avalanche danger above about 5-6000 and moderate
below Friday. Avalanche danger decreasing Saturday becoming
moderate below 7000 feet.

OUTLOOK SUNDAY...

A gradually decreasing danger is expected with the avalanche
danger remaining moderate below 7000 feet, except for slopes
receiving sunshine Sunday afternoon where a locally increased
danger is expected from triggered wet snow avalanches.

SNOWPACK ANALYSIS....

No new snow was received over the past 24 hours. Rising freezing
levels and mild temperatures Thursday through early Friday have
allowed for significant settlement in the snow pack over the past
two days. Heavy snowfall was received Monday and Tuesday at
rising temperatures with two-day total new snow amounts of 2 to 4
feet over most west slope areas with less over the east slopes
and Hurricane Ridge. Widespread avalanches occurred during the
storms of Monday and Tuesday with some accidents reported as
well. Two snowmobilers near Stampede Pass were caught when an
avalanche crossed the road Monday afternoon and buried their
sleds. They were uninjured and rescued the following day after
building a snow cave for the night. A car was also buried on
Highway 2 just west of Stevens on Tuesday. Fortunately no
fatalities occurred during this extensive avalanche cycle. The
recent heavy snowfall received Monday and Tuesday has now settled
some 6 to 12 inches with many areas reporting a thin melt-freeze
crust due to warming or sunshine Thursday. These factors are
helping to cause a gradually decreasing avalanche danger.
However, on shaded and cooler northerly facing slopes, areas of
unstable wind slab or heavy dense surface snow overlie colder
low-density snow. This is making human triggered slab releases
probable on some steeper, generally shaded north slopes at higher
elevations. Elsewhere, the settlement and/or surface crust is
helping to limit the avalanche danger, however areas of unstable
snow exist, especially on steeper shaded slopes that have
received the greatest recent snowfall earlier this week. In
addition, some strong easterly ridge top winds near the Cascade
passes Thursday and early Friday may have transported surface
snow to lee slopes building some new areas of unstable wind slab
on steep exposed westerly facing slopes where a locally increased
danger exists.

FRIDAY...

Increasing high or mid level clouds with generally light winds
and sun or filtered sunshine should allow for a slowly decreasing
danger. Mild temperatures should also allow for recent
significant new snow to further settle and consolidate.
Backcountry travelers should continue to use caution as areas of
unstable snow may linger on some steep slopes at mainly higher
elevations. Light rain or snow should develop Friday night,
however only light amounts are expected and with light winds and
this should have limited affect on the avalanche danger.

SATURDAY...

Occasional light rain or snow showers, light winds and cooling
temperatures. This should allow for any wet snow to re-freeze and
form a crust at lower elevations and on south slopes, while
generally light accumulations should not appreciably affect the
avalanche danger elsewhere.

OUTLOOK SUNDAY...

Decreasing showers with partial clearing and continued light
winds Sunday should allow for an overall decreasing danger.
However, in areas receiving strong direct sunshine Sunday
afternoon, increasing amounts of wet surface snow may make
triggered wet slides possible on mainly steeper south facing
slopes where a locally increased danger is expected.

**********************************************************

Backcountry travelers should be aware that elevation and
geographic distinctions are approximate and a transition zone
between dangers exists. Remember there are avalanche safe areas
in the mountains during all levels of avalanche danger. Contact
local authorities in your area of interest for further
information.
